# Adjacent viral growth case studies: what transfers to Makeborne

Research track: adjacent viral growth case studies. Prepared 26 October 2026, launch day of the Makeborne paid beta.

Scope: Gamma, Tome, Beautiful.ai, Framer, Durable, Carrd, Typedream, Canva (early growth), Notion templates, and the 2024–2026 consumer and prosumer apps that grew through TikTok slideshows and UGC creator networks (Cal AI, Umax and RizzGPT, Opal, multi-account slideshow networks). Base44 is included only as a benchmark.

How to read this file:

- **Verified** means a primary source or a reputable outlet that quotes the company.
- **Self-reported** means a founder or company said it and nobody audited it.
- **Vendor claim** means a tool seller published it, and the seller benefits from the number.
- **Estimate** means a third-party analyst made the number up from signals.
- **Assumption** means a number I chose for a model. It is not data.
- Data older than 2025 is labelled.
- The shared web-search budget for this run ran out before the last few checks. Items I could not confirm are marked "unverified" in section 7.

## 0. Summary

1. **Every big winner in this set turned its output into its distribution.** Gamma's shared decks, Carrd's "Made with Carrd" footer, Framer's published sites, Notion's shared templates and Canva's template pages all show the product to people who did not sign up yet. Gamma tracks "view → signup" as a core KPI. Over half of its new subscriber growth is word of mouth, meaning direct visits and branded search. Makeborne already ships three of these surfaces: a "Made with Makeborne" badge on published sites, a footer on exported books, and "Powered by Makeborne" on the client portal. In the launch build the badge has no campaign tag, so its traffic cannot be measured. It can also be removed on every plan. **The cheapest growth move on day one is to measure this loop and then strengthen it.**
2. **Micro-creators beat celebrities, and the founder onboards them personally.** Gamma works with thousands of micro-influencers. Roughly 90% of influencer reach comes from under 10% of creators. Grant Lee personally onboarded every early creator so they would "tell your story, but in their voice." Gamma also says **LinkedIn converts 4–5× better** than other platforms. That fits Makeborne's agency and freelancer buyer.
3. **Fast-growing consumer apps ran creator networks like a performance channel.** Cal AI paid about 250 creators fixed monthly retainers, spending mid six figures a month by May 2025, and reached a run rate of over $35M about 12 months after launch. Blake Anderson, involved in RizzGPT, Umax and Cal AI, reports that RizzGPT's breakout came from two creators paid $50 each, which brought 5–10M views and about 45,000 downloads on the first big day (self-reported). He ranks margins as internal UGC (50–80%) above influencers (25–70%) above paid ads.
4. **TikTok photo carousels ("slideshows") are the cheapest format to test.** In about 700,000 posts from H1 2025, TikTok carousels had about the same reach as videos and about 81% higher engagement, but roughly a third fewer shares. Multi-account, multi-language slideshow networks are documented, for example Astroapp: 385 slideshows in 105 days, more than 50M views a month across its accounts, and 5× more views after it added language accounts. Makeborne's product output (sites, decks, book pages) is ready-made slide material.
5. **Viral free usage without monetization fails.** Tome reached about 20M users, but third-party estimates put it under $4M ARR with under 2% paid conversion. It cut staff in April 2024 and shut down Tome Slides on 30 April 2025. In its own words, it needed to focus "on one role and a narrower set of use cases". Makeborne's no-free-tier decision avoids the Tome trap, but it also gives up Gamma-style top-of-funnel volume. Makeborne has to make up for that with proof before the paywall and with paid-user loops.
6. **Creator economics are a growth engine in their own right.** Framer pays template creators 100% of marketplace sales and up to 50% of referred subscriptions for 12 months. Its page lists a 2025 creator payout of $6.5M. Gamma pays affiliates 30% of first-year revenue (third-party listing) and gives 200 credits to both sides of a teammate referral. Thomas Frank made $1M from two Notion templates in 2022 (older data). When creators earn on your platform, they market your platform.
7. **SEO pages built from a gallery compound, and LLM referrals are now a real channel.** Notion's template category pages went from almost nothing to about 600 ranking pages and about 204K organic visits a month. Canva's feature pages went from about 20K to about 13M visits a month since early 2022 (Ahrefs estimates). Canva says it was a top-10 domain referred by ChatGPT, and that traffic through LLM referrals "is in double-digit percentages" (TechCrunch's wording; read as a share of traffic, not a growth rate. Corrected 26 Oct 2026).
8. **Bold claims and lucky amplification matter, but they are a lottery you prepare for, not a plan.** A Paul Graham reply to a provocative Gamma tweet set off a spike. A tweet by Kim Kardashian linking a Carrd site (30 May 2020, older data) took Carrd's daily new accounts from hundreds to thousands. Durable's "website in 30 seconds" promise was the whole pitch.
9. **The $1M ARR in 3 months math is hard, and none of these cases did it on $1,000 with no free tier.** $1M ARR is about $83,300 MRR. At an assumed blended ARPU of $42/month, that is about 2,000 paying subscribers by late January 2027. Gamma averaged roughly $167 a year per paying customer, with about 1.2% of users paying (a rough derivation: SaaStr's 600K paying subscribers and 50M users are paired here with the later $100M ARR figure, so the true ARPU at any one date is unknown). Cal AI took about 5 months to reach its first $1M in sales, while reinvesting heavily in creators. The realistic route in these cases is to prove a repeatable creator-to-paid unit at small scale, reinvest early revenue into the 10% of creators who work, and stack a new launch beat each time a "coming in days" feature ships.

**Bottom line for Makeborne:**

- Spend most of the first $1,000 on a structured micro-creator and UGC test. LinkedIn should carry the agency angle and TikTok/Instagram carousels the creator, author and small-business angle.
- Track every creator with UTM links and Whop sales.
- Make every published site, review link and exported book a measured invitation.
- Do not run undisclosed account farms. They break the FTC fake-review rule, TikTok's authenticity rules and EU consumer law, and they would destroy the trust Makeborne is positioning on.

## 2. Case studies

### 2.1 Gamma: the closest analogue, and the gold standard

**What happened (verified):**

- Founded in late 2020; launched in 2022.
- After an early Product Hunt launch that won day, week and month but then plateaued, Gamma spent 3–4 months rebuilding onboarding around one mantra: **the first 30 seconds**.
- Before the AI version, about 95% of new users dropped off before their first "aha" moment. AI drafts removed the blank page.
- After the relaunch, daily signups went from 5,000 to 10,000, then 20,000, then 50,000, with zero marketing and sales spend during that surge (SaaStr).
- Gamma reached 10M+ users in 9 months; about 80% of that growth was word of mouth (Failory, Feb 2024).
- It passed $50M ARR, profitable since early 2024, with about 30 staff (Upstarts Media, 29 Apr 2025).
- It then passed $100M ARR, profitable, with 70M users and about 50 staff, raising a $68M Series B at a $2.1B valuation (TechCrunch, 10 Nov 2025).

**Mechanisms:**

| Mechanism | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Output is distribution | Free users' shared and exported work carries "Made with Gamma"; removing it is a paid feature (Plus tier). Gamma designs every deck to be shared and tracks "view → signup" as a core KPI. Its flywheel runs Draft → Refine → Share → Return → Expand → Invite. | Failory (Feb 2024); GTMnow (14 Nov 2025); Gamma help centre |
| First 30 seconds | Generate from 10–15 words, see a full deck in about 10 s, and reach the "aha" within 30 s | Failory; Lenny's Podcast summary (13 Nov 2025) |
| Micro-creators, founder-onboarded | "Thousands" of micro-influencers; about 90% of reach comes from under 10% of creators. The CEO personally onboarded every early creator by call, brainstormed angles, and was not "super prescriptive". Creators were trained on making presentations, so their content read less like ads. | Business Insider via AOL (14 Nov 2025); Lenny's summary; SaaStr |
| LinkedIn | Lee says LinkedIn converts **4–5× better** than other platforms and is underused. A managed creator campaign in Q2 2025 ran 36 LinkedIn and newsletter posts, reached 1.36M+ impressions at a **$112 average CPM**, and used carousels, before/after posts (Gamma vs Slides) and walkthroughs. | Lenny's summary; Passionfroot case study |
| Creator ops at scale | Dozens of creators run in parallel like A/B tests, with performance-linked incentives, a library of winning hooks, and "thousands of creative pieces" tested weekly | GTMnow; Lenny's summary |
| Brand kit for creators | Brand assets published at brand.gamma.app so creators can make on-brand content | SaaStr; Lenny's summary |
| Referral credits | 200 credits for both inviter and invitee; collaboration invites bring teammates in | Failory; Gamma help centre |
| Affiliate | Run through PartnerStack (Gamma help centre confirms PartnerStack but states no rate). Third-party listings show 30% of first-year revenue and a 60-day cookie. | Gamma help centre; PartnerStack listing (rate unverified) |
| Power-user community | "Gambassadors" Slack with 1,000+ members, early access and bug reports | Upstarts; SaaStr |
| Paid guardrail | Paid should not exceed 50% of acquisitions. If it does, the organic engine is broken. | Lenny's summary |
| Margin discipline | Never ran at a negative margin to buy growth; tests 20+ models in production for cost and quality | SaaStr; Upstarts |
| Bold claim moment | A provocative launch tweet drew a public reply from Paul Graham and went viral | SaaStr; Lenny's summary |

**Unit economics signal (derived):** $100M ARR ÷ 600K paying subscribers ≈ **$167 per paying subscriber per year (about $14/month)**. 600K ÷ 50M ≈ **1.2% of users pay**. These figures come from SaaStr and may not be measured on the same date.

**What transfers to Makeborne:**

- Measure "view → signup" on every published site, review link and book.
- Founder-led creator onboarding.
- LinkedIn creators for the agency message.
- Carousels and before/after formats.
- A public brand kit.
- A paid ceiling of 50% or less.

**What does not transfer:** Gamma's free tier fed the badge loop with millions of free users. Makeborne has no free tier, so its loop runs only on paying users' output, and agencies will often remove the badge for client sites.

### 2.2 Tome: the counter-example (viral, then dead)

**Facts (verified):**

- About 20M users, most of them unpaid.
- 59 staff, with about 20% laid off in April 2024 to pivot to sales teams. The CEO said the real business was "those couple thousand sales and marketing leaders" (Semafor, 16 Apr 2024).
- Tome Slides was sunset on 30 April 2025. The team moved on to an AI CRM (Lightfield).

**Estimates (low confidence):**

- 1M users in 134 days, 10M in 10 months, 20M in 18 months.
- Monetized only after 10M users.
- Under 2% paid conversion and about $3.5M ARR on 20M users (Sacra, generated report). Another analysis puts ARR under $4M.

**Lesson:**

- Novelty-driven virality without a paying job-to-be-done does not compound into revenue.
- Built-in AI from Microsoft, Google and Canva squeezed a broad "for everyone" deck tool.
- Tome said itself that it needed one role and a narrower set of use cases.

**For Makeborne:**

- Lead with one buyer: freelancers and small agencies making client websites, which is the Studio $49 plan, labelled "best for client work". Pull decks and books along as part of the same client-delivery story, not as three separate consumer pitches.
- Promote export and ownership (GitHub export is live) because Tome's shutdown deleted unexported work. Portability is a trust message.

### 2.3 Beautiful.ai: slow and steady without an output loop

- Third-party estimates put 2025 revenue at about $13.5–14M with 93–108 staff (Latka, Dealroom; low confidence).
- The company says it has 100K+ business customers.
- It raised $45M from General Catalyst in March 2026 to push AI deck generation (Pulse 2.0, 18 Mar 2026; the article does not say whether General Catalyst led).
- Founded in 2016 (older), so this is its trajectory after a long run.
- **Contrast with Gamma:** both make decks. Gamma reached roughly 7× the estimated revenue with fewer people. The visible differences are an AI-first generation "aha", output built to be shared with a badge, and a creator engine. This is an inference from the sources, not a controlled comparison.

### 2.4 Framer: community and creator economics

**Facts (verified):**

- $50M ARR, 500K+ MAU, break-even for the past year, $2B valuation after a $100M Series D (TechCrunch, 28 Aug 2025).
- Businesses are now the majority of new customers, including 40% of a recent YC batch.

**Creator program (Framer's page, accessed 26 Oct 2026):**

- Creators keep **100% of marketplace template sales**, with a 0% cut.
- Referrers get **50% of a referred subscription for 12 months**.
- The page shows a "2025 Creator Payout" of **$6.5M** and $753K paid in November.
- A third-party tracker reported December payouts rising from $267K (Dec 2024) to $784K, and affiliates paid rising from 312 to 649.

**Mechanism:** designers sell templates and build client sites on Framer, then refer the client or the next designer. Creator income turns users into a sales force.

**For Makeborne:**

- Run an agency and freelancer partner program with a generous first-year share. Commission on a $49 Studio plan at 30–50% is $14.70–$24.50 a month.
- Later, a template and "brief pack" gallery. This is not live, so plan it.
- Agencies are both buyer and channel: every client site they publish is a showroom.

### 2.5 Durable: "a website in 30 seconds"

**Facts:**

- Soft launch in fall 2022.
- 26,000 sites created one month later.
- About 6M sites in the first year. These count builder uses, because users could build without signing up (BetaKit, 12 Dec 2023, older data).
- Freemium: 3 pages on a subdomain for free.
- Marketing on YouTube, TikTok, Facebook and Instagram, plus SEO from the founder's background.
- An independent test clocked the build at 36 seconds. It judged the result only marginally better than template starters (Big Brain).

**Mechanism:** a specific, testable time promise ("30 seconds"), a build that delivers value before signup, and demo videos where the promise is the hook.

**For Makeborne:**

- Use a concrete, honest time-and-outcome hook ("one brief → a published site, a deck and a review link") and show it on screen, uncut.
- Keep claims measurable and true. Durable's own number mixes builds with customers, which is a reminder not to inflate vanity counts.

### 2.6 Carrd: footer link, niche communities, one lucky tweet

**Facts (Fathom, 16 Aug 2021, older data):**

- 2M+ users, 3M+ sites, $1M+ ARR.
- Free plan of 3 sites; paid from $9 a year.
- Run by AJ and a partner, with contractors.
- Bootstrapped, then raised a small round in early 2021.

**Growth drivers named:**

- "Made with Carrd" footer links on free sites.
- Niche groups telling their circles. Fandom communities use Carrd bios heavily (Fanlore).
- Black Lives Matter activist pages that were widely shared.
- Kim Kardashian tweeting a Carrd site on 30 May 2020, which took daily new accounts from hundreds to thousands.

**Later:** about $2M ARR (Indie Hackers headline, undated, with "zero marketing" framing).

**For Makeborne:**

- Every published URL on a Makeborne address is a billboard. The badge must be tracked and should invite people to build their own.
- Find two or three niche communities where a Makeborne output is itself the shareable thing: local service businesses, indie authors (books), teachers and students (decks), freelancers (portfolio sites).
- Make it easy for any viral page to credit Makeborne.

### 2.7 Typedream: the quiet counter-example

- YC W20 AI and no-code site builder with "tens of thousands of active websites".
- Acquired by beehiiv in June 2024 to become beehiiv's website builder.
- **Lesson:** a capable site builder without its own distribution loop gets absorbed by a platform that owns distribution. In Makeborne's case that is the agency channel and the shareable output.

### 2.8 Canva: SEO, templates, PR, then AI and LLM channels

- **Early, older data:** Canva hit 1M users by October 2014, about a year after launch. AdNews credits SEO, branded content and tactical PR, with "no paid media, no agencies" (AdNews, circa 2016, older). Guy Kawasaki joined as chief evangelist in April 2014 (low-confidence timeline source).
- **Now (TechCrunch, 18 Feb 2026):**
  - $4B ARR, 265M+ MAU, 31M+ paid users.
  - B2B accounts with more than 25 seats grew 100% to $500M ARR.
  - Its AI websites and mini-apps tool has 10M+ MAU.
  - Traffic through LLM referrals "is in double-digit percentages" (corrected: this is a share of traffic, not a growth rate), while search is still the bigger driver; Canva claims it was a top-10 ChatGPT-referred domain, with 26M+ conversations in its ChatGPT app by October 2025.
  - Lower-priced subscriptions in Pakistan, Uruguay, Morocco and Jamaica.
- **SEO architecture (Ahrefs, updated 2 Sep 2026):** feature pages at /features/ grew from 23 to about 310 ranking pages, and from about 20K to about 13M monthly visits since early 2022 (estimate). Template pages follow one uniform layout.
- **For Makeborne:**
  - Build an examples gallery of real outputs by industry and use case, each with a "start from this brief" CTA. Design for answer engines too: clear, factual pages that an LLM can cite.
  - Regional pricing is an **owner decision**. It is relevant for the Balkans and other lower-income markets. Canva uses it to convert more payers.

### 2.9 Notion templates: creators who earn will market you

- Notion's template gallery grew out of users sharing templates. A former Head of Community built the first gallery from community templates.
- Notion's official marketplace lets approved creators sell; one version of the help page lists an 8% + $0.40 fee per sale.
- **Creator outcomes (older data):** Thomas Frank reports $1M in 2022 from two templates; Ultimate Brain alone made $760K. Easlo's figures vary by source ($239K to $500K+, low confidence).
- **SEO:** template category pages launched in late 2024 grew to about 600 ranking pages and about 204K organic visits a month (Ahrefs estimate).
- **Ambassadors:** 100+ power users in 20+ countries ran local events and translations (third-party, medium-low confidence).
- **For Makeborne:**
  - Productivity and education YouTubers sell Notion templates and drive Notion signups. The equivalent for Makeborne is creators who sell or give away "brief packs" and example projects, or who run "I built my client's site live" tutorials.
  - Short term, this is an affiliate and tutorial play. Long term, it is a marketplace (not live; a later beat).

### 2.10 Cal AI: the creator-retainer machine

**Facts:**

- Launched in May 2024. First $1M in sales by about 4 September 2024, roughly 5 months after launch.
- **Run rate over $35M** by 15 May 2025; 20–30K downloads a day; 17 staff, about half on marketing and influencer management. Bootstrapped. (Latka interview.)
- **About 250 influencers on fixed monthly fees**, not commission, priced on views and engagement. The largest earns "tens of thousands" a month for four posts. Influencer spend was mid six figures a month.
- The first signal was one influencer post that brought about 1,000 users in two days. After that they "started recruiting influencers as fast as we could". Payouts were tied to guaranteed reach. The best influencers had "very tight communities". (TechCrunch, 15 Oct 2026.)
- Monetization: a 3-day free trial; $29 a year or $2.49 a month; "hundreds" of paywall tests; 20–25% of first sessions convert to trial or paid.
- Over $30M revenue in two years; acquired by MyFitnessPal in March 2026.

**For Makeborne:**

- Copy the operating system, not the spend: many creators in tight communities, pay tied to delivered reach, ruthless reallocation to winners, and a dedicated person running creators.
- Do not copy the conversion expectations. Cal AI is a $29-a-year consumer app with a free trial. Makeborne is $20–99 a month with no trial, so expect far fewer conversions per 1,000 views and a longer consideration path.

### 2.11 Umax and RizzGPT (Blake Anderson): small budgets, huge variance

All figures in this section are self-reported.

- **RizzGPT:** paid two small creators (18–19 years old) **$50 each**. The posts drew about 5–10M views, about 45,000 downloads on the first big day, 200K that week and 500K that month. MRR peaked just over $250K (Starter Story, 29 Dec 2024).
- **Umax:** 10M+ downloads; about $500K monthly revenue, with a peak of about $700K (Startup Ideas Podcast, Nov 2024).
- **Recommended tactics:**
  - Influencer marketing plus **internal UGC**, meaning creators hired to post on brand-owned or dedicated accounts.
  - Make content in CapCut as slideshows, faceless videos or talking-to-camera.
  - Test constantly and double down on what is profitable.
  - **Reported margins: internal UGC 50–80%, influencers 25–70%, paid ads lower.**
- **For Makeborne:** a $1,000 budget can produce a breakout, but only by luck, and only if many cheap shots are taken. The structure that raises the odds is many small creators, many formats and fast kill decisions.

### 2.12 Opal: hire your best fan

- $10M ARR with 11 people; $0 to $5M with six people (Speedinvest, 19 May 2025).
- Charged from day one to learn who pays.
- A "work hours" productivity frame beat a Gen-Z mental-health frame.
- Launched US-only for a long time.
- **Creator move:** instead of brand accounts, Opal hired a TikTok creator who already used the app. She posts educational content under her own name, and her accounts went from 0 to **700K followers across platforms in a few months**. Opal **licensed an organic video she made and ran it as a paid ad**, reaching "tens of millions" (BetterLaunch summary of the Sub Club podcast, medium confidence).
- **For Makeborne:**
  - Find the first paying users who post (designers, freelancers, authors) and offer them a paid creator role.
  - Licensing the best organic post as a paid ad with the creator's handle (TikTok Spark Ads, Meta partnership ads) can work with UTM tracking and no pixel. Conversion-optimized bidding still needs pixels, and pixels need the consent banner first.

### 2.13 Multi-account and multi-language slideshow networks

- **Astroapp (Shortimize, 22 Aug 2024):**
  - One main account plus language accounts (Polish, Dutch, Portuguese, Spanish, French, Italian, German).
  - 385 slideshows in 105 days, about 3.7 a day; some accounts post 5 a day.
  - Main account: 12 posts over 1M views, a median of 37K.
  - More than 50M views a month across the network; **about 5× views after the language expansion**.
  - Format: text slides that make viewers swipe to a "reveal".
- **Format evidence (Fanpage Karma, about 700K posts, Jan–May 2025):** TikTok carousels get about 3% more reach, **about 81% more engagement** and about 82% more likes than videos, but about a third fewer shares. On Instagram, carousels get about 13% more reach than Reels and about 1.5× the engagement rate.
- **Vendor-stack evidence (low confidence):**
  - Postiz (16 Apr 2026) describes 3-slide structures (hook → value → resolution/CTA) and indie developers posting 30–50 slideshows a day across accounts with AI automation.
  - Slideshow-tool vendors claim app outcomes (for example "$80K MRR within a year") but have commercial interests. One slideshow-tool founder reported only 4 paying customers, and a marketplace listing showed $11 in 30-day revenue. Most attempts earn little.
- **UGC economics (vendor and job-board data, medium-low confidence):**

| Pay structure | Example rates | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Per video, small creators | $15–20 per video | superapp.dev playbook (undated) |
| Students, monthly | $50–200 a month plus bonus | superapp.dev |
| Experienced, monthly | $500–1,500 a month | superapp.dev |
| Per video plus view bonus | $20 per video on a new dedicated account; bonuses from $10 at 10K views up to $1.5K per video | job listing (ugcjobs) |
| Performance only | $1.00 per 1,000 views, falling to $0.50 | viral.app (1 May 2026, vendor model) |
| Milestone | $1,000–1,500 per 1M views | job listing |
| Managed LinkedIn creators | $112 average CPM | Passionfroot / Gamma (Q2 2025) |

  - Vendor CPM comparison: high-volume UGC at about $0.65–0.88 effective CPM (model), against paid TikTok ads at $8–18 and macro-influencers at $12–25 (viral.app vendor claims).
  - Healthy-campaign benchmark from superapp.dev: median 4–5K views per post, regular 10K+, occasional 100K+. **If the median is under 1K, fix the format before adding creators.**
  - Hooks that recur in these playbooks: "POV…", "This replaced…", "Nobody told me…", "If you're [X]…". The hook has to land within 2 seconds.

### 2.14 Base44 (benchmark only)

- Solo founder, building in public.

This is my recommendation for the tactics in this track. The master budget lives in the overall plan.

| Reserve | $50 | Re-runs with the best creator | — |

**Break-even guide (assumptions, not data):**

- **Blended ARPU** ≈ $42 a month, assuming a plan mix of 50% Create, 35% Studio and 15% Scale.
- **Gross margin** at full credit use ≈ 53–55%, before payment fees and hosting:
  - Create: $9 of credits against $20.
  - Studio: $22.50 of credits against $49.
  - Scale: $46.50 of credits against $99.
- **Retention:** an assumed 3-month average paid lifetime (unknown at launch).
- **Gross profit per subscriber** ≈ $42 × 3 × 0.54 ≈ **$68**.
- **Result:** keep total CAC under about $68 per paid subscriber, which means **$1,000 needs about 15 paid subscribers to break even on gross profit.**
- Re-run this weekly with real Whop data. Refunds until credits are used make early numbers noisy, so count a sale only after credits are used or the refund window passes.

**What $1M ARR requires (assumptions):** about 2,000 paying subscribers at $42 ARPU (about 1,700 if yearly-plan uptake raises effective ARPU per head; fewer if Studio and Scale dominate). At a Gamma-like 90/10 creator distribution, you need to find roughly the 10% of creators who produce 90% of reach, then fund them from revenue. The $1,000 can only find the first winners and prove the unit. It cannot buy $1M ARR.

**Content formats to start with (built from the cases):**

1. **"One brief → published client site" carousel** (TikTok, IG, LinkedIn). 6–8 slides of real screenshots. Hook types: "POV: your client wants a site by Friday", "This replaced my $X website retainer", "If you're a freelancer, steal this workflow".
2. **Before/after** (Gamma's top LinkedIn format): a messy brief or notes on the left, the finished site, deck or book on the right.
3. **Review-link workflow** (agency pain): "How I get client approval in one link instead of 40 emails." This shows anonymous comments plus the CRM. It is live, and no competitor in the earlier teardown has it.
4. **Book page-flip slideshow** (BookTok-adjacent): "I turned my notes into a book" with real pages. Keep claims honest about what the export is.
5. **Uncut real-time build video** (the Durable/Gamma "aha"): screen recording with a timer. Use the existing v14 launch film (9:16) as the brand hero, and creator UGC as the volume layer.
6. **Later beat: generated images.** Full-image slides and image-rich sites will be the strongest visual hook. Save the "watch this" wave for the day it ships, and do not tease it as available.

## 6. Risks and red lines

1. **Fake or undisclosed UGC is illegal and self-destructive.**
   - The FTC's final rule (announced 14 Aug 2024, effective 60 days after Federal Register publication) bans fake or AI-generated reviews and testimonials, reviews from people with no experience of the product, undisclosed insider reviews, and buying fake followers or views.
   - TikTok's Integrity and Authenticity guidelines (released 25 Aug 2026, effective 24 Sep 2026) allow multiple accounts only when they are not used to deceive, and they ban fake engagement.
   - The EU unfair-commercial-practices rules also require that paid promotion is disclosed.
   - **Every creator account must be disclosed** (an ad or paid-partnership label and a clear bio), and every testimonial must come from a real user.
2. **Consumer-app benchmarks overstate Makeborne's conversion.** Cal AI and Umax sell low-priced apps with trials. Makeborne is $20–99 a month with no trial. Expect a lower conversion per view and more consideration, which is why LinkedIn and agencies matter.
3. **Survivorship bias and self-reported numbers.** Most UGC and slideshow numbers come from winners or from vendors selling the tools. Several sources show most attempts earn very little.
4. **The badge loop is weaker than Gamma's.** With no free tier, only paying users publish, and agencies will remove the badge for clients. Measure it, and push the review-link surface, which agencies keep.
5. **A free tier or trial carries a cost and a Tome risk.** Each free website draft costs about $1 in provider credits. Free usage without a paying job-to-be-done did not save Tome. Any free draft or trial is an **owner decision**; if tried, gate it (for example, creator-referred traffic only, with a daily cap).
6. **Promoting features that are not live.** Generated images, custom domains, two-way GitHub sync, credit top-ups, agency tools and AI lead research must appear only as "coming" or as launch beats on their release day.
7. **Comparative claims.** Claims against Lovable, Framer and others must be measured, current and reproducible, with the method published, to stay within EU comparative-advertising rules and platform ad policies.
8. **Platform dependence.** TikTok reach and policies change. Diversify across LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube Shorts, SEO and the in-product loops.
9. **Founder time.** Gamma's founder-onboarding model costs hours. Budget about 30 minutes per creator, and stop personal onboarding after the first 20–30 creators.

**Net effect on decisions:** none of the corrections changes this track's recommendations. Two matter for execution. First, Whop's built-in affiliate program can run the partner program; configure a recurring custom partner rate for agency partners rather than relying on the global rate. Second, the "$1M ARR in 3 months" target has no verified precedent in this case set, so judge the first $1,000 on the unit test it buys, not on that headline.

### Gaps this report still has

- **Lovable, Bolt and v0 growth mechanics** are absent, although Lovable is the main competitor. Cross-reference the direct-competitor track.
- **No conversion benchmarks for paid-only, no-trial SaaS at $20–99 a month.** Every consumer case (Cal AI, Umax, Opal) had a trial or a low price. Visit-to-paid rates for card-first prosumer tools are needed to size the creator test.
- **No small-creator LinkedIn rate card.** The only LinkedIn cost datapoint is Gamma's $112 managed CPM; $50 per post for LinkedIn agency voices is an assumption.
- **No 2025–2026 data on Product Hunt, AppSumo, directories or launch communities.**
- **YouTube long-form tutorials**, a major Notion and Framer channel, are not quantified.
- **Home-market and language data** (Balkans, DACH) for B2B tools is missing; Astroapp is a consumer astrology app, so the 5× language effect may not transfer.
- **Badge-removal behaviour:** no data on what share of paying users remove a "made with" badge.
- **TikTok's branded content policy and Meta partnership-ad rules** were not checked against current pages.
- **Consent scope:** the seller is in Bosnia and Herzegovina, which is not in the EU, but EU visitors are still covered by EU consent rules for pixels. Cite the legal track for this.
